LimitGauges shows a block of water or steam quality limits as instrument faces on a cream drawing plate, one dial per limit, so a reader sees at a glance where each limit sits and which side of it passes.

- Data: a `spec` block with `"as": "gauges"` (rows `{p, v, m, hl}` as for SpecTable). Renderer: `wGauges` in `assets/src/blocks/spec.js`. Ground: a light sheet (`gauges` is in `LIGHT` in `page.js`). The list sits on a Plate (`wPlate(..., {light: true, block: ['Aquemi', caption]})`): frame, ruler ticks, registration crosses, title block.
- Markup: `.aq-plate > ul.aq-limit-gauges > li.aq-limit-gauges__item` (`is-key` for an `hl` row, `is-readout` when there is no dial). Each cell: `h3.aq-limit-gauges__p` (parameter, mono caps with a short `flow` underline), `.aq-limit-gauges__dial` (`aria-hidden`), `.aq-limit-gauges__foot` with `dl.aq-limit-gauges__spec`: "Limit" (`dd.aq-limit-gauges__v`, the full value as written) and "Basis" (`dd.aq-limit-gauges__m`, standards as `.aq-tag`, the words between them in `.aq-limit-gauges__mt`). The dial and its readout are decoration; in a dial cell the "Limit" line is visually hidden (`.aq-limit-gauges__sr`, still read by screen readers) because the chip and readout show it. The rest of the value around the readout's number and unit ("@ 25 °C (stage 1)", "(≤ 4.3 µS/cm @ 20 °C)", "(500 ppb)", or a unit too long for the readout) is shown as the readout's caption (`.aq-limit-gauges__cap.is-q`, mono 12 px, 11 px on phones, `ink-muted`, wraps) in place of "Upper limit"; the regulatory qualifiers always stay visible.
- The dial draws: a heavy `ink` scale arc with major and minor ticks and scale numbers, an inner hairline arc, the side that passes as a `flow` band (below the limit for ≤ and <, above it for ≥ and >), an `ink` pointer on the scale at the limit with a "Limit ≤ 1.3" chip, a needle from a pivot with crosshairs resting on the limit, and bottom right a large mono readout (the limit with its unit, "Upper limit" or "Lower limit" under a hairline). A unit longer than 8 characters ("mL per 100 mL") is left out of the readout; the spec list has it.
- Numbers used: only the row's own number, the first `≤ / < / ≥ / >` number in `v` outside `[placeholders]`, or a value that opens with a number (an action level, read as an upper limit). **The scale is derived from that limit alone**: 0 to the next round number (1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6 or 8 × 10ⁿ) at least 20 % above the limit (5 % for ≥ and >, so a 0.95 dryness reads on 0 to 1). The scale numbers are a ruler, not data; scale numbers next to the pointer are dropped so the chip stays readable.
- No measured value exists, so the needle rests on the limit; it never shows an invented reading. No number (a placeholder, "Not required") gives a readout cell: the same cell without the dial, the value in larger mono between dashed rules.
- Second marker: a row named "outlet" or "guaranteed" whose value has a real number in the same unit as a limit adds a dashed `flow` mark at its place on that dial's scale, with its number on an outlined chip. A placeholder adds nothing; the row stays a readout cell.
- Tokens: cells framed in `line-strong` with a registration cross in `ink-subtle`; scale `ink`, minor ticks `ink-muted`, scale numbers mono 10 viewBox units `ink-muted`; pass band `flow`; inner arc and crosshairs `line-strong`; chip `ink` with `surface` text; readout mono 24 px `ink`, caption mono caps 11 px `ink-muted`; basis sans 12 px `ink-muted`; `hl` row has a 2 px `flow` top rule.
- Desktop: three instruments across, the dial (up to 280 px) straight under the title, readout mono 24 px. Phone: two compact instruments across (dial about 135 px): scale numbers only at the two ends at about 10 px, the chip without its "Limit" word, the readout (mono 18 px) under the dial, the basis as its standard tags only. Readout cells sit in the grid like any other cell.
- Supports `fold` and `fold_noun` (default "limits"), but limits should not be folded.
- Use it for limits with numbers (conductivity, TOC, endotoxin, EN 285 steam tests, RO inlet targets). Not for design parameters or rules with few numbers: those stay a SpecTable, or ParameterCallouts when they name parts of the equipment.

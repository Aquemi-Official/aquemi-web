Where steam fails at the sterilizer, and our fix

EN 285 tests are made at the sterilizer, so a generator that passes at its outlet can still fail there.

01Non-condensable gases above 3.5 mL per 100 mL

Usual cause

Feed not degassed; air drawn into the header as it cools; no vents

What we design in

Feed degassing; vents at high points and branch ends; header kept under pressure or purged before the first cycle

02Dryness value below 0.95 (or 0.90)

Usual cause

Condensate in the header: missing traps, sagging runs, damaged insulation, dead legs, high steam velocity

What we design in

Header sloped with the flow, traps at low points, a separator at each sterilizer, lines sized on peak flow

03Superheat above 25 K

Usual cause

Dry steam reduced in pressure in one large step, or through a small orifice near the sterilizer

What we design in

Staged pressure reduction far enough upstream for the steam to return to saturation

04Condensate conductivity above limit

Usual cause

CO₂ or ammonia carried over from the feed; droplet carryover

What we design in

Feed degassing, feed conductivity limits, separator sized for peak output

05Condensate endotoxin above limit

Usual cause

Droplet carryover at peak output or low pressure; poor feed quality

What we design in

Separation sized at the highest steam velocity; feed endotoxin target set from the guaranteed reduction

06Pressure dips during cycles

Usual cause

Generator undersized for simultaneous demand; slow control response

What we design in

Capacity sized on the simultaneous peak; pressure control shown at FAT

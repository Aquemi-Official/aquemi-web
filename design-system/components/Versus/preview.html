Multi-effect or vapour compression: which fits

Multi-effect

or

Vapour compression

How it works
Plant steam boils PW in column 1; each column's vapour heats the next and condenses as WFI
A compressor raises the vapour's pressure so it condenses on the surface that boils more feed
Main energy input
Plant steam to the first column
Electric compressor; steam or electric heat at start-up
Cooling water
Needed at the final condenser; falls as effects are added
Little or none
Feed water
PW, because the first column runs hot enough to scale on silica and hardness
Often tolerates softened or reverse osmosis (RO) feed at its lower boiling temperature; confirm the maker's feed limits
Moving parts
Feed pump only
Compressor, with bearings and seals to maintain
Capital and operating cost
More effects cost more to buy and less steam to run
Compressor adds purchase cost; running cost is low where power is cheaper than steam

Choose Multi-effect when

Reliable plant steam at enough pressure and spare boiler capacity

Choose Vapour compression when

Steam is costly or limited, cooling water scarce, or power cheap, as in several provinces
